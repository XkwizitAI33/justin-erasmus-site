The 4-Stage Framework behind Prismatic Wave

By Justin Erasmus · 2026-10-31

#ai-music#hybrid-production#prismatic-wave

People hear “AI music” and picture a single prompt and a finished song. That is not how Prismatic Wave works. Every release goes through the four stages of the AlgoRythm 4-Stage Hybrid Music Framework, which I set out in a white paper in 2025. The short version: human, then AI, then human, then audience.

Human → AI → Human → Audience
AI creates possibilities. Human creativity gives them identity.

1. Concept: human vision, AI exploration

A track starts with a direction I choose: the story, the mood, the genre and the instruments. I write the raw lyrics myself. AI is used here as an analysis tool, for example to look at structure, rhyme and syllable counts, and then I rewrite the lyrics. As I told TechCentral: “You need to understand musical instruments, their characteristics and how they work together to achieve the right beat, arrangement and sound. You cannot rely on a generic prompt to replace that understanding.”

2. Generation: AI-assisted creation

Suno, and more recently Suno Studio, is the central generation tool in my workflow. Generation is iterative, not one-shot: a single song typically needs eight to 15 distinct instrumental versions, and complex tracks 20 or more.

3. Human Curation: producer intervention

This is the stage that decides whether something is music or noise. I listen, reject, combine and refine until the track matches the concept. Most of what gets generated never gets released. A single song typically goes through eight to 15 instrumental versions before one is chosen.

4. Production & Delivery: the final hybrid master

The chosen version is finished, mastered, given artwork and metadata, and released to streaming platforms. Prismatic Wave's first album, Echo Of The Streets, came out on 11 February 2025; by my own count I have since released 11 albums and 5 EPs in 13 months, plus 20 singles.

Why the framework matters

It is a way of showing the human contribution, not hiding it. That matters for rights bodies: SAMRO now requires members to disclose how much AI went into a song when they register it, TechCentral reports. And it matters for listeners, who deserve transparency about whether AI was involved in vocals, instrumentation, lyrics or other production elements.

AI creates possibilities. Human creativity gives them identity. Read more on the AlgoRythm Hybrid Music Production page.
